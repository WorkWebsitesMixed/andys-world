Use this page to revise for M2 (Weeks 1–6), in class or at home. The idea is simple. The best way to find out what you do not know is to try to write an exam question about it. If you cannot write the mark scheme, you do not know the topic yet.

<Callout type="exam" title="What M2 covers" id="why">
  The <ExamLink to="paper4">Paper 4 Section A</ExamLink> mini-quiz (M2, 30%) covers Weeks 1–6: types of structure (frame, shell, mass), triangulation, the five forces, stress and factor of safety calculations, moments and beam reactions, structural joints, wood and metal families, composites and plastics, circuit symbols, IPO, switch types, and the resistor calculation. It is 8–10 short questions in 20 minutes, and every generic word costs you the mark.
</Callout>

<Activity plan title="Write the exam, then sit the exam" time="90 min" grouping="Individual brain dump; groups of 4 for question writing; groups swap questions" materials={["Blank A4 paper", "Two pens in different colours", "Index cards or half-sheets for questions and mark schemes", "Mark scheme extracts from previous Section A papers", "Timer visible to the room"]}>

**Blank-page brain dump — individual (0–12 min):** Notes closed. For each of the four areas, write everything you remember on one half-page. When the time is up, open your notes and add what you missed in a second colour. The second-colour words are your gap list.

**Question stations — groups of 4, four stations of 12 minutes (12–60 min):** One station per area: structures, beams and joints, materials, electronics. At each station, your group writes one two-mark and one three-mark question on a card, with the mark scheme on the back, one mark point per line. Before you move on, scan your own mark scheme for generic words and replace them. Rotate every 12 minutes. At the second and later stations, first read the question the previous group left and decide whether the mark scheme is complete. Fix it if not.

**Say it out loud — groups of 4 (60–68 min):** Each person names the one topic they are least sure of. The group picks the loudest worry and one teammate explains it in two minutes without notes. If nobody can, that goes on the list for the teacher.

**Swap and sit — groups swap card sets (68–83 min):** Each group takes another group's eight cards and answers them individually, silently, in 15 minutes, as a mini M2.

**Mark and target — pairs (83–90 min):** Mark each other's answers with the card's mark scheme. Write three revision targets on a sticky note for your folder: the topic, one thing to do about it, and the day you will do it.
</Activity>

<KeyTerms terms={[
  { term: "Retrieval practice", definition: "Pulling an answer out of memory with nothing in front of you, instead of rereading notes. It feels harder than rereading and it works much better, because the effort of remembering is what strengthens the memory." },
  { term: "Mark point", definition: "One specific, creditable statement on a mark scheme. A 3-mark question has three mark points. Writing the mark scheme for your own question shows you what an examiner is actually looking for." },
  { term: "Generic vocabulary", definition: "Imprecise words such as 'strong', 'good' or 'durable' that match no mark point. Replace each with the specific property, a value or comparison, and a consequence for the product." },
]} />

<Section title="How to revise Section A" icon="🧠" id="method">

### Four topic areas, one habit

| Area | Check you can do this without notes |
|---|---|
| Structures | Classify frame, shell, mass. Name the five forces. Calculate stress (force divided by area) and factor of safety (failure load divided by working load), showing every step. |
| Beams and joints | Take moments about a support, find the reactions, check they add up to the load. Choose a joint and give a reason. |
| Materials | Sort woods, metals, composites and plastics. Give a property, a value or comparison, and a consequence. |
| Electronics | Draw the switch-operated LED circuit with correct symbols and IPO labels. Calculate the resistor, choose the next standard value, convert mA and kΩ. |

The habit is the same for all four: close the book, write what you remember, then check and mark what you missed in a different colour.

</Section>

<WorkedExample title="One question, written from both sides">

**Question (3 marks):** An LED needs 2 V and 20 mA. It is powered from a 9 V battery. Calculate the value of the current-limiting resistor and state the standard value you would fit.

**Mark scheme:**
1. Voltage across the resistor = 9 − 2 = 7 V (1 mark)
2. R = V ÷ I = 7 ÷ 0.020 = 350 Ω (1 mark, the unit conversion from 20 mA to 0.020 A is part of this)
3. The next standard (E12) value above is 390 Ω, which keeps the current below 20 mA (1 mark)

**A common wrong answer:** "R = 7 ÷ 20 = 0.35 Ω." The method marks for the formula and the 7 V are still earned, but the unit conversion is lost, and so are the other two marks. That is why the units are worth drilling.
</WorkedExample>

<Support title="If you are doing this at home">

**On your own:** do the brain dump, then write your own six questions with mark schemes (two per hardest area) and answer them again after a day. Then do the same with a friend, swapping the questions.

**What a good question card looks like:** a clear command word (state, describe, explain, calculate), the number of marks, and a mark scheme with exactly that many mark points, each one specific enough that two people would give the same mark.

**Most common ways to lose Section A marks:** a generic word, a missing unit conversion, working left out of a calculation, and an answer to a different question from the one asked.
</Support>

      "50 N"
    ],
    answer: 2,
    hint: "Take moments about support A: the load turns the beam one way, reaction B turns it the other. Then check that the two reactions add up to 100 N.",

<Section title="Homework and extension" icon="🏠" id="homework">

**Home task:** Redo your brain dump from memory after a night's sleep and compare it with the first one. Put a star on every topic that is still missing. Those stars are your revision list for the days before M2.

<Extension title="Write a full mini-paper">
Write a ten-question Section A paper covering all four areas, 40 marks in total, with a mark scheme. Swap with a friend, sit each other's paper in 20 minutes, and mark it. Which of your questions turned out to be too easy, and which were unfair because the mark scheme was vague?
</Extension>
