<Callout type="tip" title="Why this matters" id="why">
  A fair question: didn't Canva already win the CV argument? Yes — for a one-page, photo-led, visually designed document, a tool built by designers for exactly that job will usually beat hand-written LaTeX, and pretending otherwise is a losing pitch. But a CV is the easiest case for Canva and the hardest case for LaTeX: short, image-heavy, no data, no citations. Flip those conditions — a multi-page document with tables, numbers and sources that need to stay consistent when you edit — and the advantage flips too. That's the kind of document these five weeks are about.
</Callout>

<Section title="Choosing your report" icon="🗂" id="topic">

### Same tool, different subject

Your W8 session is building a Chemistry lab report skeleton — a preamble, section headings, and (from next week) tables, a figure and a bibliography. Every one of those commands works exactly the same way for a document that has nothing to do with Chemistry. That's the whole idea of a template: write the structure once, reuse it for new content.

Pick whichever of these is closest to something you're genuinely interested in — or bring your own idea if none of them fit:

| If you're interested in… | Write a… | Why LaTeX helps |
|---|---|---|
| Medicine | A short literature-review write-up on a health topic (e.g. "the effect of screen time on adolescent sleep") | Citations to real sources via `biblatex`, plus a data table if you find one |
| Management / Business | A data-driven case study or mini business plan (e.g. analysing a company's strategy, or your own idea) | A financial or comparison table via `booktabs`, cited sources for your claims |
| Engineering | A design-justification report for a project (a D&T piece, a RoboJam build, anything you've made) | A figure of your project, a table of component or material choices, cited datasheets or standards |

Whichever you pick, your report needs: an Introduction, 2–3 body sections, one table, one figure, and at least 3 cited sources, by <WeekLink grade={12} term={1} week={23}>B11</WeekLink>. From here on, this lesson uses generic placeholder text like `[your topic]` — swap in your own throughout.

### Reusing the skeleton, not rebuilding it

You don't start from a blank file. Your Chemistry report already has a working preamble — `\documentclass`, margins, encoding — proven to compile. Copy it, strip the Chemistry content, keep the plumbing.

```latex
\documentclass[12pt]{article}
\usepackage{geometry}
\usepackage[utf8]{inputenc}

\begin{document}

\section{Introduction}
% Your content goes here.

\end{document}
```

</Section>

<WorkedExample title="Weak Introduction → improved Introduction">
Weak: *"This report is about renewable energy. It is important and many people are interested in it."*

Improved: *"Colombia generated 70\% of its electricity from hydropower in 2023, a source increasingly vulnerable to drought. This report examines whether solar power is a realistic complement, using cost and capacity data from three regional projects."*

The improvement earns credibility by naming a **specific figure**, a **specific problem** the figure implies, and a **specific question** the report will answer — not a general statement that the topic "is important." The same test applies to your own Introduction, whatever the topic: replace the general claim with the number or fact that made you pick it.
</WorkedExample>

<Activity title="Set up your report" time="25 min" grouping="Individual" materials={["Laptop", "Overleaf (browser)", "Your Chemistry report project, for copying"]}>
1. In Overleaf, open your Chemistry lab report project. Use **Menu → Copy Project**, and rename the copy to your report's title.
2. In the new copy, delete everything in the body except `\section{Introduction}`.
3. Write your Introduction: 3–5 sentences, including at least one specific fact or figure — not a general statement.
4. Compile. Fix any errors before calling it done.
5. Save the project link — you'll return to this exact project every B session for the next four weeks.
</Activity>
