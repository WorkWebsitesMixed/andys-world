<Section title="Building today's topic" icon="💬" id="topic">

### Email components — what's actually different about To, CC and BCC?

**To** is the primary recipient — the person the message is for. **CC** copies other people in, and *everyone* can see who else was CC'd. **BCC** does the same, but *invisibly* — no other recipient can see who was BCC'd, including other BCC recipients. An **attachment** is a separate file sent alongside the email, not embedded in the body — and it's also the most common way malware reaches a computer, which is why the risk is worth naming explicitly.

### Spotting phishing

Revisit last unit's threats from a communication angle: how do you actually *identify* a phishing email, rather than just define the word? A generic greeting ("Dear Customer" instead of your name), urgent or threatening language ("your account will be closed in 24 hours"), a link whose domain doesn't match the organisation it claims to be from, and a sender address that looks close to genuine but isn't quite right — these are the specific tells, not "it looked suspicious."

### Internet vs intranet — what does a company intranet have that the public internet doesn't?

Restricted access. An intranet runs on the same underlying technology as the internet, but it's walled off — only people inside the organisation can reach it, and its content (internal documents, staff directories, company announcements) has no public visibility at all. The internet is open to anyone; an intranet, by design, is not.

### Environmental impact — what problems does IT actually cause?

Three, each with its own countermeasure: **energy consumption** from data centres running continuously (countered by cloud computing efficiencies — shared infrastructure used more efficiently than many small in-house servers); **e-waste** — toxic materials inside discarded devices (countered by device recycling programmes); and the **carbon footprint** of manufacturing and running hardware at scale (countered partly by energy-saving modes and longer device lifecycles).

</Section>

<WorkedExample title="Weak answer → improved answer (internet vs intranet)">
**Question:** "Explain one difference between the internet and a company intranet."

Weak: *"The internet is for everyone and an intranet is private."*

Improved: *"An intranet is a private network restricted to one organisation, built on the same technology as the internet but with no public access — only staff within that organisation can view its content, such as internal documents or announcements. The internet has no such restriction and is accessible to anyone."*

The improvement earns marks by naming **what technology underlies both** (which shows understanding, not just contrast), and by giving a **concrete example of restricted content**, not just the word "private."
</WorkedExample>

<Support title="Mark scheme — check your answers">
1. **BCC** hides the recipient list from every student, so no student can see the email addresses of their classmates — important for privacy when sending to a large group who don't need to see each other's contact details. CC would expose every address to everyone.
2. Any two of: a generic greeting instead of the recipient's name; urgent or threatening language pushing for immediate action; a link whose domain doesn't match the claimed organisation; a sender address that looks similar to a genuine one but isn't exact.
3. **E-waste** — discarded devices contain toxic materials that harm the environment if not disposed of properly. Countermeasure: **device recycling programmes**, which recover materials safely rather than sending them to landfill. (Energy consumption / carbon footprint with cloud efficiency or energy-saving modes is equally acceptable.)
</Support>
