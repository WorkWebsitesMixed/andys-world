<Section title="Mapping your report" icon="🧭" id="topic">

### The shared skeleton

Every track uses the same five-part structure — only the content inside each part changes:

| Section | Medicine | Management / Business | Engineering |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | The health question and why it matters, with one statistic | The company or idea, and the question the report answers | The project and the design problem it solves |
| **Body section 1** | What existing research/data shows | Market or competitor context | Requirements and constraints |
| **Body section 2** | Your analysis of that evidence | Your analysis (costs, risks, options) | Design choices made and why |
| **Data/Evidence** | A table of study data or comparison figures | A table of financial or comparison figures | A table of material/component choices, or a test-result figure |
| **Conclusion** | What the evidence supports, and its limits | A recommendation, with a stated risk or trade-off | Whether the design met the requirements, and what you'd change |

### Claim, then evidence — for every section

A section that only states opinions ("solar power is a good option") earns nothing without evidence attached ("...because Project C in `Table~\ref{tab:solar}` delivers the lowest cost per MW of the three"). Before writing full prose next week, plan each section as one sentence: *this section will claim X, supported by Y.*

</Section>

<WorkedExample title="Weak content plan → improved content plan">
Weak plan for a Conclusion: *"Wrap up the report and say what I think."*

Improved plan: *"Conclude that Project C is the strongest option because it has the lowest cost per MW (`Table~\ref{tab:solar}`), but note the limitation that capacity data alone doesn't account for land availability, which the report didn't investigate."*

The improved version names the **specific claim**, the **specific evidence** backing it, and a **specific limitation** — three things a reader can actually check against the rest of the report. "Wrap up and say what I think" gives the writer nothing to aim for when they sit down to write it in full.
</WorkedExample>

<Activity title="Plan every section" time="25 min" grouping="Individual" materials={["Laptop", "Your report project from B8/B9", "The section-mapping table above"]}>
For each of the five sections in your report, write **one sentence**: what this section will claim, and what evidence backs it. Not full prose yet — that's next week.

1. Introduction — the question or problem, and the one fact that makes it worth reporting on.
2. Body section 1 — the context or existing evidence you'll present.
3. Body section 2 — your own analysis of that evidence.
4. Data/Evidence — what your table or figure will show.
5. Conclusion — your claim, its evidence, and one honest limitation.
</Activity>

<Support title="Mark scheme — self-check your plan">
Read back each of your five sentences and check:

- Does it name a **specific** claim, not a general topic restated? ("Solar is interesting" is not a claim. "Project C has the lowest cost per MW" is.)
- Does it name the **evidence** — a table, a source, a comparison — not just assert the claim on its own?
- If two sentences say almost the same thing, one of your sections is redundant — merge them now, before writing full prose.
</Support>
