<Callout type="tip" title="Why this matters" id="why">
  The checklist below is deliberately structural, not subject-specific — a classmate who wrote about a completely different topic can still tell you whether your report has a caption on every table, a working citation for every claim, and a conclusion that actually answers its own introduction. That's a genuinely useful kind of feedback, and it doesn't require them to know anything about your topic.
</Callout>

<Section title="Peer review checklist" icon="🔍" id="topic">

Swap projects with a partner. Check their report against this list — tick or flag each line, and write one specific comment per flagged item:

| Check | Look for |
|---|---|
| Compiles cleanly | No errors in the log. Warnings about unresolved references (`??`) should be fixed by recompiling twice. |
| Every table has a caption and a label | `\caption{}` and `\label{}` present, and referenced from the text with `\ref{}` — not just "the table above." |
| Every figure has a caption and a label | Same check, for `\includegraphics{}` figures. |
| At least 3 citations, all resolving | No `[?]` in the compiled PDF — every `\cite{}` has a matching `.bib` entry. |
| Conclusion answers the Introduction | The question or problem raised at the start has a stated answer at the end — not a new, unrelated point. |
| One claim, one piece of evidence, throughout | Flag any sentence that states an opinion with nothing backing it. |

A comment like "`Table~\ref{tab:solar}` caption is missing — add `\caption{}` before `\label{}`" is useful. "Looks good" is not — it doesn't tell the writer what to check.

</Section>

<WorkedExample title="Vague feedback → specific feedback">
Vague: *"Your conclusion needs more detail."*

Specific: *"Your conclusion states Project C is the best option but doesn't reference `Table~\ref{tab:solar}` — a reader can't check the claim without flipping back to guess which numbers you mean."*

The specific version names the **exact section**, the **exact problem** (a missing reference, not vague "detail"), and **why it matters** to a reader. That's the difference between feedback someone can act on in five minutes and feedback that just tells them to try harder.
</WorkedExample>

<Activity title="Review, correct, export" time="35 min" grouping="Pairs, then individual" materials={["Laptop", "Overleaf — your report project", "The peer review checklist above"]}>
1. Swap your Overleaf project link with a partner. Spend 10 minutes reviewing theirs against the checklist, writing specific comments.
2. Swap back. Read their feedback on your report.
3. Spend 15 minutes making corrections — fix every flagged item, not just the easy ones.
4. Compile a final time. Confirm: no errors, no `[?]` citations, no unresolved `??` references.
5. Download the final PDF (**Menu → Download PDF**) and submit it.
</Activity>

<Support title="Mark scheme — final compile checklist">
Before exporting, confirm all of these in your compiled PDF:

- No red error panel in Overleaf.
- Every citation shows a number, not `[?]`.
- Every table and figure reference shows a number, not `??`.
- Every table and figure has a caption underneath (tables) or below (figures).
- The reference list (`\printbibliography`) appears at the end and lists every source you cited.
</Support>
