Everyone in the room understands exactly what the challenge scores before anyone touches a tool — that's the whole point of the hour.

<Callout type="tip" title="Why this session is 100% shared">
Every later session forks into electronics-pair work and design-trio work. This one doesn't — team identity, challenge analysis and the schedule aren't electronics or design, they're the frame the whole rest of the project sits inside.
</Callout>

<Section title="Agenda" icon="🗓️" id="agenda">

**1. Read the rubric together, out loud (15 min).** Don't summarize it — take turns reading sections of the actual challenge rubric (dimensions, materials, propulsion, notebook criteria, technical evaluation). Stop after each section and ask: *what does this actually require us to build or show?* Write the answers on a board — this becomes the raw material for the challenge-analysis notebook entry.

**2. Da Vinci framing (5 min).** Leonardo da Vinci sketched paddle boats and geared mechanisms centuries before motors existed — the challenge is deliberately scored to reward *wooden gear trains*, not just "does it move." That's why the rubric singles out the wooden-gear arrangement as its own line item.

**3. Team name + logo (15 min).** Fast brainstorm — everyone writes 2–3 name ideas on paper, read them all out, vote. Don't let this drag; a good-enough name beats a perfect one that eats the whole session. The logo can be a rough sketch today; it gets polished later.

**4. Member IDs and profiles (10 min).** Each student writes: name, one sentence on what they bring to the team (skill, interest, or just enthusiasm — new teammates don't need to invent expertise they don't have yet), and a photo/ID they'll wear visibly on event day.

**5. Draft cronograma (15 min).** Put the actual calendar in front of the team — this course's own T1–T8 and E1–E7 dates, the build day, the lake day (date TBD). Copy it into the notebook as your schedule. This *is* the rubric's structured-schedule item — it doesn't need to be invented from scratch, just owned and understood.

</Section>

<Section title={'What "done" looks like'} icon="✅" id="done">

A notebook page with: a team name, a logo sketch, one entry per member, a one-paragraph challenge summary in the students' own words (not copied from the rubric text), and a schedule the team can point to and explain.

</Section>
