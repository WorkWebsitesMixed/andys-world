Fix whatever the lake day revealed, close out the notebook, and rehearse the actual competition-day flow — specifically, whether **any** team member, not just the one who built a given part, can explain it under questioning.

<Section title="Triage the lake day's issues list" icon="🗂️" id="triage">

Don't just work top-to-bottom through T7's list — sort it first. For each issue, place it in one of three buckets:

1. **Fixable in the time left, worth fixing** (safety-relevant, or cheap and high-impact).
2. **Fixable but not worth it** (real but minor, would eat time better spent elsewhere).
3. **Not fixable by competition day** — this becomes a **documented known limitation**, not a hidden one. Evidence of learning from failure is explicitly rewarded; an honestly flagged, unresolved issue with a clear explanation of *why* it wasn't fixable in time scores better than either hiding it or pretending it doesn't exist.

Write the bucket for each issue directly in the notebook next to the item — that categorization is itself useful evidence of engineering judgment, not just busywork.

</Section>

<Section title="Targeted fixes (20 min)" icon="🔧" id="fixes">

Work bucket 1 first. Split by track as usual (electronics issues to the pair, mechanical/waterproofing issues to the trio), but this is also a good moment for cross-track help if one group finishes early — a novice helping re-test a fix they didn't build is exactly the kind of shared ownership T2's systems-overview walkthrough was setting up for.

</Section>

<Section title="Reflection section" icon="💬" id="reflection">

10 min, individually written, then shared. Each student writes, without conferring first: **one thing they're proud of, one thing that surprised them.** Push for specificity — "I'm proud we finished" is weaker than "I'm proud the gear ratio math I did by hand at T4 turned out to match the render." Share out loud after everyone's written theirs; hearing several different specific answers is itself useful notebook material — a genuine team reflection, not five copies of the same sentence.

</Section>

<Activity title="Judges'-checklist dry run" time="15 min" grouping="Whole team, mock interview">

Not just a checklist — an actual mock interview. Print or display the full technical-evaluation rubric line by line (structure/finish in wood, waterproofing, gear arrangement, free motor/servo movement off-load, RC operation, autonomous operation, flotation, stability, the physical autonomous-mode switch, effective water displacement, steering precision). Then:

1. **Randomly assign** each rubric line to a team member — deliberately **not** always the person who actually built that part. Use a shuffled deck of cards, drawn names, whatever is quick.
2. That student has **30 seconds** to explain, out loud to the rest of the team, what that line item means for your specific boat and whether you meet it.
3. If they can't, that's real signal — not a failure of that student, but a gap in how the team shared understanding. Fix it now with a 2-minute explanation from whoever *does* know, not by re-assigning the question to someone easier.

This is where T2's systems-overview walkthrough and T5/T6's whole-team sessions either pay off or reveal they didn't reach everyone — better to find that out now than in front of the actual judges.

</Activity>

<Section title={'What "done" looks like'} icon="✅" id="done">

A notebook that's genuinely finished — every rubric line item traceable to a session and a person, with known limitations documented rather than hidden. A team that's rehearsed explaining their own boat under mild pressure, not just built it.

</Section>
