<Activity title="Stage 3 — static float with full payload" time="25 min" grouping="Whole team" materials={["A tub, sink or shallow pool big enough for the hull", "Ruler", "Straightedge or a level app"]}>

You need a tub, sink, or shallow pool big enough for the hull — this doesn't need open water. Load the boat with **everything it will carry at the competition**: battery, motor, board, both switches, wiring — not a partial load, since a partially-loaded boat trims and floats differently than the real one.

**1. Waterline and freeboard — the actual measurement.** With the boat floating and settled (give it 10–15 seconds to stop rocking), hold a ruler vertically at the **bow, midships, and stern** and measure from the waterline up to the gunwale (deck edge) at each point. Record all three numbers. The pass criterion is **≥15 mm freeboard at the lowest of the three points** — not an average, the *minimum*, since that's where water would come in first in a chop.

**2. Trim — fore-aft and port-starboard.** Two checks:
- **Fore-aft:** does the bow or stern sit noticeably lower? Compare your bow/stern freeboard numbers — a difference of more than a few mm is worth addressing.
- **Port-starboard:** lay a straightedge or a phone with a level app flat across the deck, port to starboard. It should read close to level. A consistent lean is a mass-distribution problem, not a hull problem.

**3. Paddle immersion depth.** With the boat settled, look at where the paddle blades sit relative to the water — the target is roughly **⅓ of the blade radius submerged at rest**. Too deep and you're dragging extra water for no thrust gain; too shallow and the blades slap instead of push. **This connects directly back to T4:** immersion depth is set by the paddle axle's height above the hull bottom in the parameter file — if it's visibly wrong here, that's a real design parameter to revisit, not something to shim around with tape. Note it in the notebook either way.

**4. If something fails, fix the actual cause, don't mask it.** In order of how disruptive the fix is:
- **Trim off / freeboard uneven:** move existing mass (battery position is usually the easiest lever) before adding anything new.
- **Sitting low overall:** you're heavier than the design assumed — either remove non-essential mass or, if that's not possible, this is evidence for the mass budget section that your original estimate was optimistic (valuable, honest notebook content).
- **Paddle immersion wrong:** flag for a T4 revisit before the lake day if there's any time left; otherwise document it as a known limitation.

</Activity>
